Projects

A common thread runs through our projects, from dance to menopause: finding biomarkers that tell us who is on an unfavourable aging trajectory, and what we can do to help.

Each project is a cohort we scan with our quantitative MRI protocol. The software we build along the way is on our Tools page.

CIRM

Quantitative MRI of neurovascular disease in cardiovascular patients

Do cardiovascular diseases leave a mark on the brain before cognitive decline appears? CIRM compares people with coronary artery disease (CAD) to healthy controls with a one-hour quantitative MRI session: cerebral blood flow (CBF), cerebrovascular reactivity, pulsatility, oxygen extraction (OEF), myelin (MTsat) and iron (QSM, R2*). We found demyelination and higher iron in white-matter watershed areas in CAD, worse after bypass surgery (CABG) than after stenting (PCI) and with more severe disease (SYNTAX score), in participants considered cognitively normal. Heart and Stroke Foundation Grant-in-Aid, 2017–2021 · started April 2018.

NeuroDance

Can dancing change the trajectory of brain aging?

A pilot trial in which adults over 60 dance for six months. Learning choreography, coordinating movement, navigating space and adapting to partners challenges body and brain at once. We compare dance with a combined exercise and cognitive-training program and with a stretching and toning program, measuring CBF, OEF, CMRO2 and cognition before and after.

MENO-IRM

Brain health at menopause

Before menopause, sex hormones appear to protect the cardiovascular system; afterwards, cardiovascular risk rises and some women may become more vulnerable to cognitive decline. We follow women aged 30 to 60, from premenopause to the first years after menopause, with and without hormone therapy, to image the effects of estradiol, arterial stiffness and inflammation on the brain. CIHR, 2022–2023 · started May 2022.

COV-IRM

The impact of COVID-19 on brain health

Using quantitative MRI to measure how COVID-19 infection affects cerebral blood flow, vascular function and oxygen metabolism. Started February 2022.

CHD-IRM

Brain health in congenital heart disease

Adults born with congenital heart disease now live long lives, but the lifelong effects of altered circulation on the brain are poorly understood. CHD-IRM applies our quantitative MRI protocol to measure their brain’s vascular and metabolic health.
